STIX Luxury

An online storefront for a senator-wear tailoring house in Douala. Customers browse in English or French, upload a full-body photo and see themselves wearing an outfit before they buy, then order straight into the shop's WhatsApp with every detail filled in.

The hard part: AI images cost money per use, so an open try-on button is an open wallet. I built a server-side quota: three previews per customer a month, a daily cap for the whole shop, and failed attempts refunded automatically.

02Computer vision

FaceSort

Drop a thousand event photos in. FaceSort finds every face, works out who is who without a single reference photo, and gives each guest a folder of only their pictures. Guests open one link with a passcode, or take a selfie and let it find them.

The hard part: grouping faces with no labels to learn from, then letting a person correct mistakes with one tap. It runs two ways: a double-click on a laptop for tonight's event, or Docker with automatic HTTPS on a server.

Saint Mary Campus Services

04Language preservation

Douala AI

A chatbot that talks in English, French and Duala, the language of the Douala people. Almost no Duala exists in digital form, so I made some: I ran OCR over a scanned 1972 Duala–French dictionary and turned a paper book into something an AI model could use.

The hard part: a language with no training data is a sourcing problem. I had to get clean text out of a fifty-year-old scan, then ground the model in it so it answers in real Duala.

PythonFastAPIClaude APIOCR
05Creative tooling

Motion Studio AI

A motion-graphics studio inside a browser tab, with nothing to install. Describe the animation, adjust it on a timeline, then export it with real transparency as ProRes 4444 or a PNG sequence that drops straight into DaVinci Resolve.

The hard part: transparency. Normal browser video export flattens it and editors show a black box. Getting a true alpha channel out of a web page in a format professionals accept is most of the work.

06● In use

Base Global Logistics — Company Portfolio

During my first-year industrial attachment at Base Global Logistics Ltd, a freight-forwarding company in Douala, I built the company portfolio that presents its air and sea cargo, customs clearance and warehousing services to clients. It went through rounds of review with the Head of Sales and was approved and adopted by the company.

The hard part: it was not my business and not my voice. I learned how a logistics company works from the field first, then revised until the people who sell those services said it represented them.

About

Who is building this

I'm Ty Asher Ringnyu Ginti Njo, a developer in Douala, Cameroon, studying for a Bachelor of Engineering in Data Science and AI Engineering at Saint Mary’s University Institute. I build software the way a tailor works: it has to fit the person who ordered it, and it has to survive being worn.

Most of what I make starts as somebody's real complaint. A tailor losing sales because customers cannot picture themselves in the outfit. A photographer spending a weekend sorting event photos by hand. Students with nowhere to send their laundry.

The AI work interests me most where it meets things that are local and overlooked: a language with no training data, a shop with no online presence, a payment method the big platforms ignore.

I'm open to freelance and contract work: web applications, AI integrations, e-commerce and automation for businesses that need the practical version.
